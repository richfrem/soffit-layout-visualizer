1. EXPERT BUILDING SCIENCE (The “Physics” Authority)

Primary Source: Joseph Lstiburek (Building Science Corporation)

  • Video: “To Vent or Not to Vent” by Building Science Corp
    • Watch on YouTube
    • Key Insight: The “Don’t Suck” Rule. If exhaust > intake, the attic depressurizes and sucks conditioned air from the house.
    • Key Insight: “Wash the Deck.” You must wash the entire underside of the roof deck with cold air to prevent ice dams.
  • Book: Builder’s Guide to Cold Climates (2022 Edition)
    • Key Insight: In cold climates, balanced ventilation is safety-critical. Snow acts as an insulator (R-2/inch), requiring soffit flow to flush heat even in insulated attics.
  • Article: “Interview with Dr. Joe Lstiburek — The Ventilation Debate Continues”
    • Read on Energy Vanguard
    • Key Insight: Warns against over-ventilating the living space (62.2 rates) but mandates balancing attic pressure to prevent moisture pull.

2. REGULATORY & LEGAL (The “Hard Law”)

Source: British Columbia Building Code (BCBC 2018/2024)

  • Link: BC Building Code - Section 9.19. Roof Spaces
  • Section 9.19.1.2 (Ventilation Ratios):
    • Requirement: 1/300 ratio mandated.
    • Distribution: Requires 25% of vents at the TOP and 25% at the BOTTOM.
  • Section 9.19.1.3 (Clearance/Airway):
    • Requirement: Mandates a 63mm (2.5 inch) airspace between insulation and roof sheathing at the wall plates.
    • Application: Proves that intake vents located mid-roof (SV-50s) fail to provide the mandatory airway at the eaves.
    • CRITICAL LINK: This section directly defines the violation shown in “Dead Zone” photos of the soffits.

Source: CSA Standards (National Standard of Canada)

  • Standard: CSA A93:19 (R2024) - Airflow ventilators for the unoccupied spaces of buildings
    • CSA Group Store Link
  • Clause 7 (Weather Infiltration):
    • Vents are tested for rain/snow entry under specific airflow directions. Using an exhaust vent (SV-50) as an intake creates “Vacuum Infiltration” risks not covered by the certification.
  • Clause 9 (Marking):
    • Manufacturers must mark the Net Free Area (NFA), legally forcing the roofer to do the math.
